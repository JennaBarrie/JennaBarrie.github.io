Project Overview

This project explores medical vision-language representation learning by fine-tuning a CLIP ViT-B/32 on a curated subset of the ROCO dataset. The goal was to improve semantic alignment between radiology images and their corresponding clinical text descriptions using contrastive learning.

Using PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers, a training pipeline was built to process medical image-caption pairs and optimize a shared embedding space where related images and text are positioned closer together. The model was trained for multiple epochs on a reduced dataset to demonstrate feasibility under compute constraints while preserving meaningful learning signals.

Evaluation was performed using image-text similarity ranking, where the model's ability to match radiology images with correct captions was assessed against plausible medical alternatives. Training dynamics were analyzed through loss curves, including both raw and smoothed visualizations, to demonstrate convergence behavior and stability during fine-tuning.

Overall, the project demonstrates how general-purpose vision-language models like CLIP can be adapted to the medical domain, highlighting their potential for radiology-based retrieval, diagnostic support, and clinical AI applications.

CLIP Training Loss — ROCO Subset

This plot shows loss at each logged training step during fine-tuning of the CLIP model on the ROCO subset.

  • Each point represents how wrong the model was at a specific training step
  • The loss measures how poorly image-text pairs are aligned
  • Lower loss = better alignment between medical images and captions

Smoothed CLIP Training Loss

A cleaned version of the training curve where short-term noise is removed using a rolling average. The blue line shows the raw loss; the orange line shows the smoothed trend.

  • Small batch sizes, noisy gradient updates, and variability in image-caption difficulty all contribute to raw fluctuations
  • Smoothing reveals the true learning trend — a clearer downward slope
  • Supports stable convergence and confirms training is not oscillating or diverging

Key Findings

Loss dropped sharply from ~1.31 to ~0.70 in the first 100 steps, showing rapid early learning.
Smoothed curve confirms stable convergence with no divergence or oscillation across 500+ steps.
Demonstrates CLIP can be effectively adapted to the medical domain under compute constraints.

Applications

Radiology-based image retrieval, diagnostic support systems, and multimodal clinical AI — areas where improved image-text alignment directly supports clinical decision-making.
